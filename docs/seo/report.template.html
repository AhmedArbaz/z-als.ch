Summary

The site now gives Google, and every visitor, what they need on the first request: a home page in each of the four languages, a page for each resort transfer, a distinct title and description on all 102 pages, structured data that describes the business, and a booking path that no longer flashes while it loads. The work went live on {{date}}. The crawl, the checks and the desktop figures below were measured on https://z-als.ch itself; the mobile figures compare the old and the new build side by side on one machine.

About position one. Nobody can promise it. Google ranks on how well a page answers the search, how many trusted sites link to it, what customers say about the business, and what competitors do. This report removes what was holding the site back and builds the pages for the searches that bring bookings. What moves rankings next is mostly outside the code: Google reviews, a verified Google Business Profile and links from other sites. Those are in the roadmap, with who does what.

What we found on 4 October

  • The German, French and Italian home pages did not exist as pages. They were the English page re-written by script behind ?lang=de, and each one named the English page as its canonical address. Google could only ever see English.
  • Titles and descriptions repeated. 39 of 67 pages shared a title with another page, 31 titles were longer than Google shows, 3 pages had no description and 58 had one that was too short or too long. Service pages carried brand wording with no search term in it.
  • 28 of 33 articles in German, French and Italian were still in English while declaring themselves translated, which tells Google the language tags cannot be trusted.
  • No page for any resort. The 30 transfer pages of the old site redirected to the home page, so a search such as "Zurich to Davos transfer" had nothing to land on.
  • Structured data was thin. One block of company data on some pages, none on the home page or booking pages, and it used a type that schema.org does not define.
  • Nothing was compressed or cacheable. The home page sent 112 KB of HTML and about 3.4 MB in total on a phone on every visit, and its main photo was a 2400 px image fetched from another company's server.
  • A loading flash. For about half a second the closed mobile menu painted over the top of the page before the page had assembled itself, then the hero jumped in. Measured shift on the home page: 0.18 on desktop; on the booking page: 0.52.
  • No analytics and no consent notice. Nothing recorded which pages or which searches led to enquiries.

Keyword research

How it was done

In a real browser, with language and region set for each market (English, German, French, Italian). We read Google's autocomplete suggestions, the "People also ask" questions, related searches and who holds page one, 82 results pages in all (16 to 25 per language), and the title, headings, length and structured data of the pages that rank. German and English were read on google.ch; French and Italian on google.com with Switzerland as the region, after google.ch asked for a CAPTCHA. A CAPTCHA was never bypassed. When one appeared part-way through the competitor study we read DuckDuckGo's results for that part, so those competitor ranks are DuckDuckGo's. The result is 427 candidate phrases across the four languages in keyword-plan.csv, each with intent, a volume proxy, a difficulty proxy and the page it is assigned to.

What the results pages show

  • Generic airport searches ("zurich airport transfer") are led by marketplaces and the airport authority, with paid ads above them. Small Swiss operators still hold many page-one places, some with pages of fewer than 1,000 words.
  • Pages that rank for a resort route state the distance, the drive time, the pick-up details, the cost and a comparison with train and taxi, and are mostly 1,000 to 2,500 words long.
  • Only 2 of the 28 competitor pages we opened had a price in the title. The new descriptions state the fixed starting fare, the flight tracking and 24-hour dispatch that the ranking pages repeat, and the route pages show the full fare table.

Each page targets one main phrase, so no two pages compete for the same search. Titles are 58 characters or fewer, descriptions between 125 and 160.

What was built

Pages and content

  • German, French and Italian home pages at /de, /fr, /it, translated before the page is sent, each with its own title, description, canonical address and FAQ.
  • {{route-count}} route pages and four route overviews, with the fare table, the drive, the pick-up details and a FAQ for each resort.
  • All 44 journal articles now exist in each of the four languages (11 per language), and the four journal indexes are translated.
  • A unique, keyword-led title and description on every page, social preview image on every page.
  • The home page links to the route pages, the journal and the route overview; the footer carries "Routes and fares".

Structured data

  • Every page carries the business block (Kloten address, languages spoken, service area, social profiles); inner pages add the breadcrumb path, and the home, booking and legal pages the website entry.
  • Article markup on journal posts, service and fare markup on route pages, FAQ markup on the home pages and route pages (36 pages).
  • No star ratings. Rating markup is only added once real, visible customer reviews exist; invented or unseen ratings can lead to a manual penalty.

Technical foundation

  • One address per page: /index.html, /quote.html, trailing slashes and /en all redirect to the clean address. Old-site links and campaign tags (utm_*, gclid) survive the redirect.
  • Every language version of a page names the others and each of them names it back, which is what Google needs to show the right language to the right searcher.
  • Sitemap with language alternates and real modification dates; robots.txt keeps only the private /api/ out.
  • Text is compressed and cached with validators: the home page HTML travels as about 19 KB (about 145 KB uncompressed, more than the 112 KB before because the page now carries a FAQ, route and journal content).

Loading and first impression

  • The page is held back until it has assembled, so nothing paints out of order. The closed menu can no longer appear over the hero. On desktop the layout shift fell from 0.18 to 0 on the home page and from 0.52 to 0 on the booking page; on a phone it stays under 0.03 on every page tested.
  • The hero photo is hosted on the site at 1600 px and preloaded instead of fetched from another server at 2400 px.

Analytics and consent

  • Google Tag Manager GTM-MKMXMR2X on every page, behind a consent banner in four languages that follows Google Consent Mode v2. No analytics cookies are set until the visitor accepts; the privacy notice was updated to match.
  • The booking steps, enquiries, payments, calls, e-mails and WhatsApp clicks are sent as events without any personal data.

The side-by-side copies do not load Tag Manager, which adds about 117 KB and a short task after the page has loaded on the live site. On the live site itself the home page scored {{live-mobile-home}} on mobile (median of 3 runs on 6 October against one run on 4 October); the live runs are kept in docs/seo/after.

The SEO score is now 100 on every page measured; the home page and the booking form scored 92 before. What visitors experience is measured by Core Web Vitals on real devices, which Search Console reports after about four weeks of traffic. That report, not these lab figures, is the one to watch.

Checks that run before every release

  • seo.test opens every page in the sitemap and checks title and description length and uniqueness, the canonical address, that each language link is returned by the page it points to, a single Tag Manager snippet, that the structured data matches the visible text, and that the page is written in the language it claims. All {{pages}} pages pass on the live domain.
  • fares.test checks the price engine and that the prices written on the restored pages match it.
  • glitch-probe loads the home, booking and legal pages on throttled desktop and mobile and fails if anything paints before the page is assembled or the layout moves.
  • gtm.test and events.test check that consent starts denied, the banner works in four languages, no analytics cookies are set after "Decline", and the booking events carry no personal data.

Analytics: one step is still yours

Google Analytics is not receiving data yet. The tag manager container GTM-MKMXMR2X loads on every page, but the published version of the container contains no tags, so nothing is sent to Google Analytics. Adding the Google Analytics 4 tag in Tag Manager and publishing it takes about ten minutes and needs your Google login. The steps are in ga4-gtm-setup.md. Until then no traffic, search-to-enquiry or booking numbers can be reported, so the 30-day review below depends on it.

Roadmap: the next 90 days

New pages are usually crawled within days once the sitemap is submitted. Long, specific searches such as a resort route are the most winnable and tend to move first. Broad searches such as "zurich airport transfer" are held by marketplaces and the airport authority and typically take months and outside links to move.

Days 1 to 30

Get seen and measured

  • Add z-als.ch to Google Search Console, submit sitemap.xml, request indexing for the four home pages and the nine English route pages.
  • Publish the Google Analytics 4 tag in Tag Manager and mark generate_lead and purchase as key events.
  • Claim and verify the Google Business Profile for the Kloten address.
  • Ask every customer for a Google review, with the short link.
Days 31 to 60

Read the first real data

  • Open Search Console's Performance report: which searches show impressions, which pages have a low click rate. Rewrite those titles first.
  • Track about thirty phrases from the plan each week, English and German first.
  • Register in Swiss business directories and with hotel concierge and event partners that can link to the route pages.
  • Publish two journal articles a month from the plan's gaps.
Days 61 to 90

Add weight and reach

  • Win links from partners to route pages: hotels, wedding venues, travel agencies, corporate travel pages.
  • Add route pages that Search Console shows demand for. Candidates to test, only lightly covered by the research so far: St. Anton, Gstaad, Verbier, Grindelwald, Flims.
  • Show real Google reviews on the site once there are some. Do not expect star ratings in search results from this; Google rarely shows them for a company's reviews of itself.
  • Re-run the audit and Lighthouse and compare with this report.

Limits of this report

  • No ranking is promised. The report lists what was done and measured, and what is likely to move results next.
  • Keyword volumes are proxies. Rankings of competitors were read once, on 4 October 2026.
  • Lighthouse scores are single-machine lab results. The mobile Performance score varies by 10 points or more between runs.
  • Google Analytics shows no data yet. Results in terms of visits, enquiries and bookings cannot be reported until the tag in Tag Manager is published.
